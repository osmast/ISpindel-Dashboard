                                              1. Project Brief


            Background
            In the self-study phase you taught yourselves the brewing process, and in the exchange
            with a beer expert you clarified open questions and collected input. Later in the semester,
            each of you will brew your own beer on a scale of approx. 20 litres together with a
            brewmaster. During fermentation, an iSpindel floats in each fermenter and transmits
            measurement data such as gravity and temperature at regular intervals.

            Measured values are only valuable if they are stored reliably, assigned to the correct batch
            and remain accessible later. Closing exactly this gap is the purpose of this project.

            What the system is useful for
            Without such a system, fermentation is usually checked by taking samples and measuring
            the gravity by hand. This only provides individual values, takes time, and every sample
            means opening the fermenter, which increases the risk of contamination. A monitoring
            system replaces these spot checks with a continuous record and creates benefits on
            several levels:

             • Process control during fermentation: The course of fermentation can be followed at
                any time without opening the fermenter. Deviations such as a stalled fermentation or an
                unsuitable temperature are noticed early, and the end of fermentation can be
                determined reliably from a stable gravity. This is the basis for deciding when the beer is
                ready for the next process step.

             • Quality and traceability: Every batch is documented with its complete fermentation
                data. In the food industry, such batch records are the foundation of quality assurance
                and make it possible to trace afterwards what happened in a batch.

             • Learning from comparisons: Because all experiments are stored in the same structure,
                the influence of recipe, yeast or temperature on the fermentation can be compared.
                Over several semesters, a data set grows that can be evaluated systematically.

             • Transfer to industry: Capturing sensor data, storing it in a structured way and making it
                usable via a dashboard is the same principle as in industrial food production. What you
                build here on a small scale is a building block of a digital production environment.

            Assignment
            The client commissions you, as the contractor, to develop a fermentation monitoring system
            on a Raspberry Pi. The system receives the data of the iSpindels via MQTT, stores it per
            experiment in a database and makes it available via a web dashboard in the local network,
            both live and historically.

            You work alone or in a group of two. Each participant receives one iSpindel and one
            Raspberry Pi from the start of the project; a group of two develops one common system
            and submits its deliverables together. In a group of two, everything said in this assignment
            about «your beer» and «your iSpindel» applies to the beers and iSpindels of both members.
            During the fermentation phase, your system also integrates the other iSpindels in use, so
            that all running fermentations can be followed in your dashboard.

            The central objective is a database that can be used for many different experiments. It must
            make it possible to evaluate and compare fermentations after the fact, for example the
            beers from this semester with each other or with batches brewed by future cohorts.

            The users of the system, such as brewmasters and students, are not programmers. They
            need a user-friendly dashboard that they can operate without any programming knowledge:
            starting and ending experiments, following the fermentation, comparing experiments and
            downloading data.

            Learning objectives
            After completing this project you are able to:

             1. design a data model for process data from several sensors and experiments and justify
                your design decisions,

             2. implement a data pipeline on a Raspberry Pi that receives sensor data via MQTT and
                stores it persistently and reliably,

             3. develop a user-friendly web dashboard that visualises live and historical fermentation
                data and allows users to manage, compare and export experiments,

             4. verify a system systematically against a requirements specification and interpret the
                data of a real fermentation.

                           1.1. Positioning in the Automation Pyramid

            The automation pyramid describes the hierarchical structure of automation systems in
            industry. It ranges from the field level (sensors and actuators) through the control level to
            the supervisory level (MES) and the enterprise level (ERP).




                                       Figure 1: Automation pyramid (Copilot 2025)

            Your fermentation monitoring system covers the lower three levels:

             • Field level: The iSpindel acts as a sensor and provides raw data from the fermentation
                process.

             • Control level: The Raspberry Pi receives the data via MQTT and processes it locally.
                Since the system only monitors and does not control (no actuators), this level is limited
                to data acquisition.

             • Supervisory level (MES): The database stores the measurement data in a structured
                way and assigns it to experiments (batches). This corresponds to a simplified precursor
                of a Manufacturing Execution System.

             • Enterprise level (ERP): An integration into an ERP system is conceivable in future, for
                example for batch management or quality assurance. It is not part of this project.

            In your concept, assign each component of your own architecture to one of these levels.
            This shows that you understand where your solution sits in an industrial data chain.

            Network and access
            The Raspberry Pi needs internet access at all times, because the iSpindel data is
            transmitted via the internet.

            The dashboard, however, only needs to be accessible within the local network, via http://
            hostname:port (for example http://dfp-pi-01:8080). It must not be published to the internet:
            no port forwarding, no tunnels, no public hosting.

            Technology choice
            You are free to choose programming languages, frameworks, databases and dashboard
            tools. Whatever you choose, you justify it in your concept: which alternatives you
            considered and why your choice fits the requirements best.

            Scope

            In scope

             • Receiving, storing and assigning the measurement data of all iSpindels in use

             • Managing experiments via a user-friendly dashboard

             • Live and historical visualisation, comparison and export of the data

             • Operation of the application, the database and the dashboard on the Raspberry Pi

            Out of scope

             • Controlling the fermentation (e.g. temperature control, actuators)

             • Changes to the iSpindel hardware or firmware and its calibration

             • Access to the dashboard from outside the local network and user authentication

             • Integration into ERP or other enterprise systems
